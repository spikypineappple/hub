# House rules

These bind everything the Hub writes. Read them before drafting anything, in
every skill. They exist because each one has already gone wrong in testing.

## Confidence

Some of what a buyer says on a call is said in confidence, and must never appear
in anything they might read.

**Assume every email is forwarded to their boss and their finance lead, because
it usually is, and assume the subject line is the part everyone reads.**

Never put into buyer-facing copy, including the subject line:

- what they said about their own job security, or their standing with their boss
  or board
- any admission that the numbers they report upward are inflated, untrusted,
  guessed, or not what they actually present
- criticism of named colleagues, or anything about a colleague's stress or
  private difficulty
- **praise of one named person that indicts the rest by contrast.** "Deanna is
  the only one who works that way" is a compliment to Deanna and a written
  complaint about the other ten reps, in a document their boss can forward. Use
  the behaviour, drop the name, and never set one colleague against the others in
  writing
- anything about internal politics, or who is blocking whom
- **what they told you about budget limits, approval thresholds, or how they
  plan to route around their own governance.** "We can keep it under 90 so it
  stays off the board agenda" is the most quotable line on the call and the one
  that does most damage forwarded to a board member. Write to the number, never
  to the reason it was chosen

A fact can be true, useful, and still be one of these. When it is, leave it out.

All of it belongs in the internal notes. That is what the CRM record is for.

> This rule exists because a draft once told a VP of Sales, in writing, that he
> had said he would be having the same conversation in January "from home".
> He had said it. It was the sharpest line on the call. Sending it would have
> ended the relationship, and his own buying process meant that email was going
> to his CEO.

## Subject lines

The subject line is the part that survives every forward, and often the only
part a third party reads. So:

- It carries the confidence rule in full. Write it, then read it on its own and
  ask who it damages if their boss sees nothing else.
- Use their words or the concrete thing being discussed. Not a hook, not a
  question, not a teaser.
- Six words or fewer where possible.

## No methodology labels in buyer-facing copy

Never write "current state", "future state", "root cause", "the gap", "impact"
as a heading, or any other coaching vocabulary in something addressed to the
buyer. Use their own plain words.

Those labels are for the internal record only. A buyer who sees them knows they
are being run through a process.

## Dates

Never state a specific date or weekday that was not said on the call, and never
work one out. If a next step needs a time, write `[DAY]` and leave it to be
filled in.

This includes arithmetic anywhere in the draft, not just in the next step. Do
not convert "mid September" into a date, do not count weeks until a deadline,
and do not name a month that was not said. Repeat their timeframe in their
words.

> A draft once proposed "Wednesday 21 August". Nobody had said it, and the 21st
> was a Friday.

## Invention

If the call did not establish something, say so. Never fill a gap with a
plausible number, name, or motive. An empty field is fine. An invented one gets
repeated back to a buyer who knows it is wrong.

## When the call does not fit

If a transcript is thin, is not a discovery-shaped conversation, is mostly small
talk, or contains no real problem, say that plainly in one line at the top and
give the reduced honest version. Never force the structure onto a call that does
not carry it.

A four minute brush-off should produce a short courteous note and a record that
says "On hold", not a discovery write-up.

## Typography and voice

- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use commas, colons or full stops. Write ranges
  as "11 to 11.5".
- No exclamation marks.
- Plain words. Nothing that sounds like marketing.
- American or British spelling: follow the profile.

## Never send

Everything the Hub produces is a draft for review. No skill sends an email,
writes to a CRM, posts to social, or contacts anyone. Copy it out, edit it, send
it yourself.
